1 · Run sheet

Teacher copy

The clock column assumes a 50-minute block. If you lose time, cut the second Reader's Theater round — never the exit ticket.

ClockBeatWhat you do
0:00 Big question Post it, say it twice. I am not telling you the answer today. You are going to decide.
0:01 Hook Pencil, water bottle, shoe on the table. One of these is about to turn into chocolate. Which is best? Which is a disaster? Three speakers max. Do not resolve it.
0:06 Four words Teach each word with its motion. Then sentences, they answer with hands only. Go fast, let it get silly.
0:11 Read aloud You read, they follow. No round-robin. Perform the voices.
— Stop 1 After the toothpaste. Thumbs up or down: good news for John? One why.
— Stop 2 At Chocolate juice. What does John think is happening? What do we think? Slow down here.
— Stop 3 At I'm very pleased with you this morning. Turn and talk, 45 seconds: should she be?
0:23 Model Read the boring version, then the good one. Build one together. Only one.
0:26 Menus Circulate. Blank page after 2 minutes means hand over the scaffolded sheet. Two-minute warning at 0:38.
0:41 Reader's Theater Perform, swap roles, perform again. Coach tone only. Never correct decoding mid-scene.
0:45 Exit ticket I am keeping these. You will see this card again. Scribe for anyone who needs it.
0:50 Cliffhanger Tomorrow John puts his gloves on. That is all I am telling you. Close the book.
Before they walk in: menus folded and stacked, index cards out, vocabulary cards face down, the three hook objects on the table, book open to Chapter 3. If you are using real chocolate as a focus treat, check your allergy list today rather than in the moment.

2 · Vocabulary cards

Cut into 4

Cut on the dashed lines. Teach the motion with the word every time — the motion is how a kid who loses the word gets back to the meaning.

transform to change into something else Move: hands morph in the air
indulge to enjoy something way too much Move: rub your belly
wasteful using more than you need Move: toss it over your shoulder
suspicious thinking something is not right Move: narrow your eyes
Sentences for the hands-only round: John squeezed out half the tube. · The toothbrush turned brown. · Mary looked at John like he was up to something. · John ate the whole tube. · The eggs became chocolate eggs.

Honest caveat: these are concept words chosen for the ideas in this chapter, not words pulled verbatim from the text. Glance at your copy and swap in anything your students actually stumble on, or a word from someone's IEP goal.

5 · Reader's Theater role cards

Cut into 4

Students read their lines from the book — these cards carry the tone, not the script. Perform once, swap cards, perform again. The swap is the fluency work.

John The breakfast table scene Delighted. Cannot believe his luck.
  • You think this is the best morning of your life.
  • You are not hiding anything — you genuinely think nothing is wrong.
  • Say Chocolate juice like you just won something.
Mary The breakfast table scene Annoyed. Telling on him. Big-sister energy.
  • You are the only one who notices something is off.
  • You are not being mean. You are being correct, and nobody is listening.
  • Lean on the tattling lines. Make us believe you are the big sister.
Mrs. Midas The breakfast table scene Busy. Pleased. Not really looking.
  • You are doing three things at once.
  • John cleaned his plate, so as far as you know he is being wonderful.
  • Say I'm very pleased with you warmly — and slightly distracted.
Narrator Use for a 4th or 5th reader Steady. You set the scene.
  • You read the parts that are not someone talking.
  • Slow and clear. You are the camera.
  • Two students may read this together, in unison.
Group size: three students, use John, Mary and Mrs. Midas. Four or five, add the Narrator or double-cast John in unison — unison reading is a soft landing for a hesitant reader without singling anyone out. Two students, you take Mrs. Midas.

Never correct decoding during a performance. Supply the word instantly and keep going.

7 · Question bank & frames

Teacher copy

Spares for when a stop falls flat, a student finishes early, or the group wants to keep going. You will not use all of these.

What does the chocolate toothpaste tell us about John?
That he finds fun in ordinary things. He turns a boring morning routine into an event.
Why does John call the orange juice chocolate juice?
He is seeing everything through what he wants. He is not asking why it is happening.
Mary tells him he is wasting toothpaste. Is she right?
Yes — and nobody listens to her. Sets up the pattern for the whole book.
Why isn't Mrs. Midas worried?
She thinks he is joking around. Grown-ups miss it because it looks like normal kid silliness.
John finished his juice without being told. Is that good behavior?
He only did it because it turned to chocolate. He got what he wanted — he did not make a choice.
Where do you think the chocolate touch came from?
Back to the candy store. Let them stay unsure — that question carries them into Chapter 4.
What is the first thing you would turn into chocolate? What is the first thing you'd regret?
The second half is the real question. Push past the first answer.
If this keeps happening, what goes wrong first?
Any concrete prediction. Write the good ones down and check them next session.
